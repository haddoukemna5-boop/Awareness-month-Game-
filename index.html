Challenge 3 · The classified vault room

Three documents, three classifications

Société Générale Investment Banking sorts information into three levels — C1 (Internal), C2 (Confidential) and C3 (Strictly Confidential). Read each document below and decide where it belongs.

The three levels, in a nutshell
C1 · Internal — for use within the bank; low sensitivity, no client or deal detail.
C2 · Confidential — client-identifiable or business-sensitive data; restricted circulation.
C3 · Strictly Confidential — market-sensitive, non-public information (e.g. live M&A, trading strategy); the smallest possible circle of people.

Q1 — Is this recording real, or a deepfake?

This isn't far-fetched
In 2019, fraudsters used AI-cloned audio of a UK energy firm's CEO to trick a manager into wiring over €200,000. In 2024, a Hong Kong finance employee joined what looked like a video call with several colleagues — including the CFO — and authorised a transfer of roughly HK$200 million. Every person on that call was a deepfake. Fabricated "announcements" about deals or acquisitions can spread just as fast, and can move markets or breach disclosure rules if anyone acts on them or repeats them as fact.

ULVN
How a Caesar cipher works
A Caesar cipher encrypts a word by shifting every letter a fixed number of places through the alphabet. Here, each letter of the real word was shifted 3 places forward (A→D, B→E, C→F … wrapping Z back to A). That's how R became U, I became L, S became V, and K became N.

To decrypt it, do the reverse: shift each letter 3 places backward through the alphabet (U→T→S→R, and so on for the rest).
